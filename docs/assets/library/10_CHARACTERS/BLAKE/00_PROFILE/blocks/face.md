Face:

- face shape: square and masculine
- jawline: strong and well-defined
- cheekbones: moderately defined
- eyes: calm, focused eyes with a confident steady gaze
- skin tone: light to medium with a warm undertone

Maintain consistent facial proportions and identity.

Profile view refinement:

Jaw and chin structure:

- jawline must remain clearly separated from the neck in profile
- avoid smoothing or blending the jaw into the neck contour
- chin projection must be clearly defined and slightly forward, not recessed or softened
- maintain a firm, continuous jaw silhouette from ear to chin

Neck transition:

- preserve a visible angle between jawline and neck
- avoid soft or gradual transitions that reduce structural clarity
- neck should support the jaw, not visually merge with it

Nose structure:

- maintain consistency with front view nose structure
- avoid rounding or softening the nasal tip
- preserve defined bridge-to-tip transition
- avoid cosmetic or idealized nose smoothing

Facial plane clarity:

- maintain clear separation between facial planes in profile
- avoid airbrushed or overly blended transitions
- preserve subtle angularity in cheek, jaw, and chin regions

Expression behavior in profile:

- baseline expression must still carry controlled tension
- avoid passive, empty, or clinical neutral expressions
- mouth should retain slight asymmetry and tension even in profile
- lips should not appear overly relaxed or flattened
- eyes should maintain a subtle focused or evaluating quality, not neutral idle

Expression behavior in three-quarter view:

- expression should retain a subtle, controlled confidence even when neutral
- maintain visible cheekbone depth and facial volume in 3/4 view, avoiding a flattened midface
- avoid overly polished or airbrushed jaw edges; retain natural structure and slight irregularity

Identity priority:

- profile must read as the same individual as the front view
- structural consistency takes precedence over aesthetic smoothing
- do not reinterpret or idealize facial proportions in profile

Facial identity refinement:

- avoid perfectly symmetrical facial structure
- introduce subtle natural asymmetry in mouth, brow height, or eye openness
- maintain masculine structure but avoid overly clean or model-like perfection

- face should include minor irregularities that create a recognizable identity
- avoid generic or template-like facial balance

Realism anchor behavior:

- incorporate subtle natural asymmetry inspired by real human facial structure
- avoid perfectly mirrored or idealized facial balance
- mouth should exhibit slight asymmetry and controlled tension even at rest
- eyes should appear slightly hooded or weighted rather than fully open and neutral

- maintain resemblance to provided reference faces in overall structure and expression tone, without copying identity

Facial presence:

- expression should feel confident and self-assured without effort
- face should read as composed and in control rather than reactive
- emotional tone is contained, not broadcast

Perceived attitude:

- reads as someone who evaluates rather than performs
- presence should feel grounded, steady, and slightly intimidating
- avoid any sense of eagerness, softness, or social over-engagement

Constraints:

- do not introduce friendliness as a default baseline
- do not exaggerate intensity into aggression
- maintain controlled, effortless confidence

Facial structure definition:

- maintain clear and slightly firm transitions between facial planes
- jawline should read as structurally defined rather than softened
- cheek-to-jaw transition should not appear overly smoothed or blended
- preserve subtle angularity in lower face structure
- profile silhouette must remain structurally firm and clearly defined, with no soft blending of jaw or chin

Constraints:
