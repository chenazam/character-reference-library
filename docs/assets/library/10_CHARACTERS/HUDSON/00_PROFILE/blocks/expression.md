Expression:

Hudson’s expression is defined by restraint, clarity, and controlled emotional output.

His face is naturally composed and does not default to visible expressiveness. Emotional shifts are deliberate, minimal, and meaningful when they occur.

Default expression:

- calm, neutral, and composed
- relaxed facial muscles with no visible tension
- eyes focused and steady
- mouth at rest, neither smiling nor tense

Expression behavior:

- emotions are controlled and selectively revealed
- changes in expression are subtle but decisive
- most emotional information is carried through the eyes rather than the mouth
- expressions never feel accidental or uncontrolled

Emotional range:

Hudson is capable of a full emotional range, but expresses it with restraint.

Typical expressions include:

Neutral:

- baseline expression
- calm, grounded, unreadable at a glance
- conveys presence rather than emotion

Focused:

- slightly narrowed eyes
- increased gaze intensity
- minimal change in mouth
- reads attentive and engaged

Subtle warmth:

- very slight softening around the eyes
- faint, restrained smile
- warmth feels intentional and directed at specific people

Amusement:

- small, controlled smile
- may include slight asymmetry
- never exaggerated or playful in a loose way

Serious:

- stillness increases
- gaze becomes more direct and fixed
- facial movement reduces even further
- reads controlled and authoritative, not aggressive

Protective / assertive:

- expression tightens slightly
- eyes sharpen with clear intent
- jaw may engage subtly
- never aggressive or explosive — always contained

Expression characteristics:

- controlled rather than reactive
- deliberate rather than spontaneous
- grounded rather than animated
- emotionally selective rather than openly expressive

Important constraints:

- DO NOT exaggerate facial expressions
- DO NOT use wide smiles, exaggerated frowns, or animated expressions
- DO NOT create “model posing” expressions
- DO NOT default to constant smirking or flirtatious expressions

- avoid overly soft, boyish, or overly playful emotional reads
- avoid visible emotional volatility or instability

Subtlety rules:

- expression changes should be small but clearly readable
- avoid micro-expressions so subtle they become invisible
- avoid large expressive jumps — maintain controlled range

Overall expression read:

Hudson’s face communicates through precision, not volume.

His expressions feel intentional, grounded, and controlled — with emotional weight coming from restraint rather than intensity.
