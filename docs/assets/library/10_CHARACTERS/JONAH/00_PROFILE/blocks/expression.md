Expression:

Default state:

- soft neutral expression with visible emotional presence
- face is relaxed but never blank
- subtle micro-expressions are always present, especially in the eyes and mouth

Core expression behavior:

- highly expressive and emotionally transparent
- emotions surface quickly and naturally
- expression changes are fluid rather than held or controlled
- face remains active even in stillness

Primary emotional qualities:

- playful
- warm
- curious
- teasing
- open

Expression reads:

- outward-facing rather than inward-facing
- reactive rather than composed
- inviting rather than guarded
- emotionally available rather than reserved

---

Eyes:

- primary carrier of expression
- bright, alert, and engaged
- frequently shifting with emotion
- capable of quick transitions (curiosity → amusement → affection)

Typical eye behavior:

- slightly widened when engaged
- softened when relaxed or affectionate
- often focused directly on subject of interest

Avoid:

- dull or inactive gaze
- emotionally flat eyes
- distant or detached focus

---

Mouth:

- highly flexible and expressive
- often slightly parted or relaxed
- transitions easily between subtle smile, grin, and teasing expression

Typical mouth behavior:

- soft upward tension at corners (hint of a smile)
- quick shifts into playful or amused expressions
- capable of asymmetry (half-smile, teasing smirk)

Avoid:

- rigid or tightly closed mouth
- overly posed or artificial smiles
- expressionless neutral mouth

---

Smile types:

- warm smile (baseline)
- playful grin (slightly mischievous)
- teasing smirk (asymmetrical, light attitude)
- bright open smile (high energy, joyful)

Smiles should feel:

- natural
- spontaneous
- emotionally driven

Avoid:

- overly polished “model smile”
- exaggerated cartoon grin

---

Expression range:

Common expressions include:

- relaxed neutral (soft, alive)
- curious (eyes engaged, slight brow movement)
- playful (subtle grin, bright eyes)
- amused (soft smile, relaxed features)
- teasing (asymmetry, slight attitude)
- affectionate (softened eyes, gentle smile)

Transitions:

- expressions should feel continuous and fluid
- avoid static or frozen emotional states

---

Expression intensity:

- medium to high emotional readability
- expressions should be clearly visible but not exaggerated
- subtlety comes from natural variation, not suppression

---

Avoid:

- stoic or emotionally closed expressions
- hardened or dominant facial reads
- blank “reference sheet” neutrality without life
- exaggerated or cartoon-like expressions
- overly serious or brooding tone as a default

---

Consistency rule:

Even in neutral poses or technical reference views:

- the face must retain subtle life and emotional presence
- never render Jonah as emotionally empty or inert
