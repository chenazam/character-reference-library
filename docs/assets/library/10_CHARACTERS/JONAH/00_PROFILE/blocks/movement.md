Movement:

Core movement quality:

- light, fluid, and expressive
- movement originates from the whole body, not isolated joints
- transitions are smooth and continuous rather than abrupt

Overall read:

- energetic but controlled
- playful but precise
- spontaneous but physically confident

---

Weight and balance:

- weight shifts easily and frequently
- often rests more on one leg rather than evenly balanced
- center of gravity feels mobile rather than planted

- subtle bouncing or buoyancy may be present in relaxed states

Avoid:

- heavy or grounded stance
- static, evenly distributed weight
- stiff or locked posture

---

Upper body movement:

- shoulders relaxed and mobile
- arms rarely hang completely still

Typical behavior:

- small gestures while speaking or reacting
- hands naturally find movement (adjusting clothing, light gestures, expressive motion)
- elbows remain soft, not rigid

Avoid:

- rigid arms at sides
- minimal or “frozen” upper body

---

Torso behavior:

- slight twists and turns occur naturally
- body rarely faces perfectly straight unless intentionally posed
- subtle curvature and asymmetry are common

Avoid:

- perfectly straight, squared-off alignment
- mannequin-like symmetry

---

Lower body movement:

- steps are light and controlled
- feet placement feels intentional but effortless

Typical behavior:

- slight inward/outward foot variation depending on stance
- relaxed knee bend in idle poses
- occasional crossing or shifting of legs

Avoid:

- heavy footfalls
- locked knees
- overly wide, planted stance

---

Idle stance behavior:

- rarely completely still
- micro-movements present:
  - weight shifting
  - subtle posture adjustments
  - small head or shoulder movement

- may lean slightly, tilt hips, or shift balance casually

---

Energy profile:

- baseline energy is present even in stillness
- movement feels responsive to environment and people

- quick to react physically:
  - turning toward stimuli
  - shifting posture in conversation
  - expressive body language

---

Emotional movement link:

- movement reflects emotion clearly

Examples:

- playful → lighter steps, more movement in hands and shoulders
- curious → slight forward lean, head tilt
- affectionate → softened posture, closer body orientation

---

Physical identity:

- trained body awareness (gymnast / dancer influence)
- strong coordination and balance
- capable of dynamic movement, even if not actively performing

---

Avoid:

- heavy, grounded, or dominant movement patterns
- minimal or reduced motion
- rigid, posed, or static body language
- overly aggressive or forceful physicality
