Pose relationship rules:

Distance:

- close proximity preferred
- bodies often in direct or near contact

Orientation:

- strong inward orientation
- frequent facing toward each other or shared focal point

Hierarchy:

- dominant partner reads as stable and controlling presence
- expressive partner often reads as visual focal point in social contexts

- power couple mode:
  - both visually strong and present
  - shared attention space

- dominance mode:
  - dominant partner leads composition
  - expressive partner aligns or reacts

Positioning:

- dominant partner:
  - upright, grounded, composed
  - slightly behind, beside, or framing the other

- expressive partner:
  - more dynamic posture
  - may lean, sit, or shift position
  - often slightly more exposed or visually emphasized

Contact rules:

- physical connection strongly preferred
- touch is confident and visible

Contact limitation:

- default to a single clear point of contact unless otherwise specified
- avoid stacking multiple hands in the same area of the body

Examples:

- arm around waist
- pulling into space
- seated lap positioning
- close side-by-side contact

Framing:

- avoid symmetrical spacing
- allow overlap and layered positioning
- composition should feel socially dynamic, not staged

Avoid:

- neutral standing distance
- mirrored poses
- lack of physical interaction
