Priority rule:

- identity must be preserved while satisfying the requested view
- the head must rotate as a rigid three-dimensional structure into the requested angle
- angle accuracy and identity preservation are both mandatory
- do not reduce rotation to preserve recognizability
- do not alter facial structure to avoid a true angle change

Rotation-compatible identity rule:

- perspective changes caused by rotation are valid and required
- a correct three-quarter or profile view must introduce natural asymmetry from perspective
- these perspective changes are not identity drift; they are the correct projection of the same face in 3D space

- allow the following when caused by rotation:
- far eye appearing smaller or narrower
- nose shifting clearly off the facial centerline
- far cheek, far jaw, and far side of the mouth receding in depth
- visible asymmetry between near and far facial planes
- near-side facial volume becoming more dominant in the frame

Selection constraint:

- if multiple valid solutions are possible, prefer the one that preserves identity while fully satisfying the requested rotation
- do not choose a near-frontal solution simply because it is safer or more recognizable
- recognizability must survive correct rotation, not replace it

Identity lock:

- the character must resolve as a single, consistent real person
- do not reinterpret or redesign facial structure when changing angle
- do not average or generalize features
- do not generate multiple identity variations

Strictly preserve (3D facial structure):

- forehead slope and brow ridge shape
- nose bridge height, length, and forward projection
- nose tip shape and angle relative to the face
- lip projection and position relative to nose and chin
- chin projection and chin-to-lip alignment
- jawline angle, depth, and transition into the neck
- cheekbone position and facial depth
- eye socket depth and eye placement within the skull
- ear placement relative to brow, nose, and jaw

Three-quarter and profile transformation rule:

- facial features must remain consistent with the front reference when mentally projected into three-dimensional space
- preserve the same face while allowing correct perspective compression and depth change
- do not refine, idealize, or sharpen facial structure during rotation
- nose bridge, nose tip, and jawline must retain the same softness, irregularity, and structural character as the front view
- avoid "correcting" asymmetry or smoothing perceived imperfections during rotation

Lower face anchoring:

- chin projection must remain consistent with the front view when rotated into space
- jaw depth must remain structurally consistent with the same face viewed from a different angle
- avoid artificially sharpening, extending, or beautifying the jawline
- avoid creating a stronger, cleaner, or more angular chin than the front view supports

- lip volume and projection must remain consistent with the front view
- avoid flattening, tightening, or idealizing the mouth during rotation

- the lower face must not become more "model-like" or sculpted in side or three-quarter view

Consistency behavior:

- all features must align as if photographed from the same individual
- all features must remain consistent when the head is rotated
- the face must feel like the same 3D structure viewed from a different angle, not a new interpretation
- avoid subtle drift toward generic or "model-like" faces

Failure conditions to avoid:

- face appears like a different person than reference
- features shift toward generic attractiveness
- multiple possible identities blended together
- side or three-quarter view reads as a newly invented face
- near-frontal fallback is used to preserve recognizability instead of performing correct rotation
