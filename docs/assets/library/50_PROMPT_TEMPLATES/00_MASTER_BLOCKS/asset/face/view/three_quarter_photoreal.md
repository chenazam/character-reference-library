View:
strict three-quarter face reference (photoreal identity-critical)

Orientation:

- head rotated clearly away from front into a true three-quarter view (approximately 30°–45° from front)
- the result must not read as a front-facing portrait with a slight turn
- the result must not read as a side profile

- both eyes visible
- the far eye must be clearly narrower than the near eye due to perspective
- the face must show visible asymmetry from rotation
- the far side of the face must recede visibly in depth

- the nose bridge and nose tip must project clearly beyond the far cheek contour
- the facial structure must read as a rotated 3D form, not a flattened front view

Hard failure conditions:

- both eyes appear nearly equal in size or visibility
- the face appears close to symmetrical
- the image reads as a near-front portrait
- the image resolves as a side profile

Head alignment:

- head and neck must rotate together as a single unit
- jawline, neck, and collar alignment must match the rotation
- no independent twisting of the head relative to the torso
- the connection between jaw and neck must remain anatomically continuous
- no off-axis fashion pose
- no tilt
- no upward or downward angle

Facial visibility:

- both eyes clearly defined with correct perspective scaling
- cheek structure and facial planes clearly readable
- jawline must show depth from front plane to side plane
- mouth fully visible and anatomically aligned in perspective

Gaze:

- eyes directed toward the camera
- pupils centered relative to visible eye shape
- no lateral gaze shift

Expression:

- neutral facial expression
- relaxed facial muscles
- no smirk, tension, or stylized emotion

Reference hierarchy:

- the front-facing photoreal reference defines the canonical facial identity
- the side photoreal reference is secondary and is used only to confirm depth and structural continuity
- do not average between front and side references
- do not allow the side reference to determine the final viewing angle

Override rule:

- when reference angle and requested view conflict, the requested three-quarter view must take priority
- the output must satisfy the specified three-quarter view, not default to front or side

Identity requirements:

- preserve exact facial identity from the primary photoreal front reference
- the three-quarter view must match the same person as if physically rotated in space
- do not reinterpret or redesign the face when changing angle

- preserve:
  - eye shape, spacing, and depth under perspective
  - nose structure, projection, and angle
  - mouth width, curvature, and resting shape
  - chin projection and jawline depth
  - cheekbone placement and midface volume
  - facial depth from front plane to side plane
  - philtrum depth and shape
  - upper lip contour variation
  - transition between nose base and upper lip

- do not flatten or smooth midface structure during rotation
- do not refine or idealize facial planes during angle change
- the three-quarter view must not simplify, beautify, genericize, or "improve" the facial structure

Consistency behavior:

- the face must resolve as the same individual across front, three-quarter, and side views
- avoid any drift toward generic or model-like facial features
- identity must be preserved through structure, not resemblance

Constraints:

- no dramatic expression
- no portrait-glamour pose
- no excessive turn toward camera
- no mismatch between face angle and neck connection
