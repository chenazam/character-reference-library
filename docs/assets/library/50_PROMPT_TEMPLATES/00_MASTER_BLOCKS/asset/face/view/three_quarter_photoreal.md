View:
strict three-quarter face reference (photoreal identity-critical)

Orientation:

- clear perspective distortion: one side of the face closer to the camera, the other side receding, visible depth difference across the face
- nose clearly offset from the center of the face due to angle
- head rotated to a true three-quarter angle (~35–45 degrees from front)
- face clearly turned away from the camera, not facing forward, not a frontal view
- rotation must be visually obvious and structurally readable
- the face must not resolve as near-frontal under any circumstance
- both eyes remain visible, but the far eye must be visibly narrower and more compressed by perspective
- the nose must be clearly offset from the facial centerline
- the far cheek, far jaw, and far side of the mouth must visibly recede in depth
- the near side of the face must dominate the visible facial volume
- camera positioned slightly to the side of the subject, capturing the face from an off-center angle

Hard failure conditions:

- both eyes appear nearly equal in size
- the nose appears close to centered
- the face reads as a front portrait with slight turn
- the face reads as a front portrait with head tilt
- facial symmetry remains nearly frontal
- the angle is ambiguous or shallow

Head alignment:

- head and neck rotate together as a single unit
- shoulders and collar alignment must support the head rotation
- no tilt
- no upward or downward angle
- no fashion-portrait twisting or glamour posing

Gaze:

- gaze remains natural to the rotated head position
- do not force the eyes to square back toward the camera if that reduces the visible three-quarter angle
- priority is correct head rotation, not frontal eye contact
