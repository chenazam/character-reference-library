Environmental interaction:

- the character should be naturally integrated into the environment rather than isolated from it
- incorporate subtle interaction with surroundings (e.g. walking, shifting weight, adjusting clothing, touching nearby surfaces, or engaging with the setting)

Behavior:

- posture should reflect an ongoing action or moment rather than a held pose
- avoid static "standing for camera" body language
- allow slight asymmetry and natural imbalance consistent with movement or activity

Framing and depth:

- depth of field should feel natural and scene-driven rather than portrait-style subject isolation
- avoid excessive background blur that separates the character from the environment
- the environment should remain readable and contextually present

Goal:

- the character should feel like part of a living scene rather than the sole subject of a photograph
